SAM FAULKNER - BIOGRAPHY

Sam Faulkner is a British photographer and the founder of Print Swap. He
began in documentary photography after travelling to Afghanistan in the
1990s, work that won him the Ian Parry Award. Over the following decades
his projects have explored conflict, memory and the human cost of
political violence, including his work on the war on drugs.

Unseen Waterloo, his portrait project imagining the faces of Waterloo,
examined how history remembers the ordinary soldiers of a battle fought
before photography could record them. There's no photograph of an
ordinary soldier from that war.

Across this work runs a sustained interest in what photographs preserve,
and how they're valued. Print Swap traces back to a conversation with
Magnum's David Hurn at the World Press Masterclass in 2001, about a
lifetime of swapping prints with other photographers. The idea sat for
twenty-five years until it resurfaced, looking at a gap on Sam's own
wall.
